Conference Championship Tiebreaker Rules

How each conference's published tiebreaker procedure works, and exactly how SportsSimulate's 2025 season simulation evaluates every step - written as reference material independent of any particular week's scenarios.

B12 Big 12

  1. 1

    Head-to-head record

    If the tied teams played each other this season, that result decides it directly - among three or more tied teams, this is each team's combined record against just the others in the tie.

  2. 2

    Combined record against common conference opponents

    Each tied team's record against every conference opponent that all of them played is compared, treating those shared opponents as a single group.

  3. 3

    Record against the next-highest-placed common opponent, working down the standings

    Starting from the best-placed team the tied group has in common, each tied team's record against that opponent is compared - if that's still even, the process repeats against the next-best common opponent, and so on down the standings.

  4. 4

    Strength of schedule

    The aggregate difficulty of each tied team's full-season schedule, based on opponents' win percentages.

  5. 5

    Total wins

    Whichever tied team has more wins on the season overall, conference games included.

  6. 6

    Team Rating Score

    The conference's own computer rating system, combining results and schedule strength into a single score.

    Not simulated: SportSource Analytics' Team Rating Score isn't a feed this app ingests, so this step is skipped and the tiebreaker falls through to the next one.

  7. 7

    Random draw

    If every step above still leaves teams tied, the tie is broken by a random draw - in practice, a coin toss.

Official source: Big 12 Tiebreaker Procedures

SEC SEC

  1. 1

    Head-to-head record

    If the tied teams played each other this season, that result decides it directly - among three or more tied teams, this is each team's combined record against just the others in the tie.

  2. 2

    Combined record against common conference opponents

    Each tied team's record against every conference opponent that all of them played is compared, treating those shared opponents as a single group.

  3. 3

    Record against the next-highest-placed common opponent, working down the standings

    Starting from the best-placed team the tied group has in common, each tied team's record against that opponent is compared - if that's still even, the process repeats against the next-best common opponent, and so on down the standings.

  4. 4

    Strength of schedule

    The aggregate difficulty of each tied team's full-season schedule, based on opponents' win percentages.

  5. 5

    Scoring margin

    Point differential in games among the tied teams (capped per game so a single blowout can't dominate the comparison).

    Point differential can only be measured from games that have actually been played, so this step - and any scenario above that depends on it - can stay genuinely uncertain until later in the season. The SEC has also never fully published its capping formula, so this is our best reconstruction rather than a verified match.

  6. 6

    Random draw

    If every step above still leaves teams tied, the tie is broken by a random draw - in practice, a coin toss.

Official source: SEC Football Tie-Breaking Process

AAC American (AAC)

  1. 1

    Head-to-head record

    If the tied teams played each other this season, that result decides it directly - among three or more tied teams, this is each team's combined record against just the others in the tie.

  2. 2

    CFP rankings (or the equivalent computer-ratings composite before the committee's rankings begin)

    The higher-ranked team in the most recent College Football Playoff committee rankings - or, in weeks before the committee publishes rankings, a composite of computer rating systems.

    Approximated using conference strength of schedule - real CFP Selection Committee rankings and computer-rating feeds aren't ingested, so this isn't the true official metric.

  3. 3

    Combined record against common conference opponents

    Each tied team's record against every conference opponent that all of them played is compared, treating those shared opponents as a single group.

  4. 4

    Overall winning percentage

    Winning percentage across all games played, not just conference games - so it can differ from total wins when teams have played a different number of games.

  5. 5

    Random draw

    If every step above still leaves teams tied, the tie is broken by a random draw - in practice, a coin toss.

Official source: American Conference Football Championship Tiebreakers

CUSA Conference USA

  1. 1

    Head-to-head record

  2. 2

    CFP rankings (or the equivalent computer-ratings composite before the committee's rankings begin)

    The higher-ranked team in the most recent College Football Playoff committee rankings - or, in weeks before the committee publishes rankings, a composite of computer rating systems.

    Not simulated: CFP Selection Committee rankings aren't ingested, so this step is skipped and the tiebreaker falls through to the next one.

  3. 3

    Computer rankings composite

    An average ranking across several computer rating systems (e.g. Connelly SP+, SportSource, ESPN SOR, KPI).

    Not simulated: depends on external computer rating feeds (SP+, SportSource, ESPN SOR, KPI) that aren't ingested.

  4. 4

    Computer rankings composite (fallback)

    A narrower backup composite (just two of the same systems), used only if the full composite above is still tied.

    Not simulated, for the same reason as the composite above.

  5. 5

    NCAA Academic Progress Rate

    A measure of eligibility and retention for the team's players - very rarely the deciding step, but written into the conference's official policy as a last resort.

    Not simulated: NCAA Academic Progress Rate data isn't ingested.

  6. 6

    Random draw

    If every step above still leaves teams tied, the tie is broken by a random draw - in practice, a coin toss.

Official source: CUSA Football Championship Game Tiebreaker Policy

The two division champions are then seeded 1-2 by overall conference record, with head-to-head, then a computer-rankings composite (not simulated - pending a ratings feed), then a coin toss as fallbacks if that's still tied.
